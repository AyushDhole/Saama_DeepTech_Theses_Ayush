5Evaluation

How I’d evaluate them

The same five steps for every company in every thesis. Each step has a clear output, and the scorecard below turns them into one view. A score organises my judgment; it never replaces it.

Step 1

Founder and team

Integrity, relevant experience, speed of execution, hiring ability, founder–market fit and willingness to learn.

Reference calls, including ones the founder didn’t give me

Step 2

Problem, product and market

Is the problem painful, frequent and expensive? Is the product clearly better than alternatives? TAM, SAM and SOM, customer budgets, adoption barriers and timing.

A bottom-up market size I can defend

Step 3

Traction and unit economics

Revenue growth, retention, repeat purchase and organic demand. How money is made: gross and contribution margin, CAC and LTV.

Unit economics rebuilt from invoices

Step 4

Financial health, moat and scale

Burn, runway, debt and working capital. Competitors, switching costs, network effects, proprietary data and IP. Can it grow without costs and headcount rising as fast?

A view on runway and what protects the lead

Step 5

Valuation, risk and diligence

Entry price, dilution, future rounds, exit scenarios, ownership and returns for a $100M fund {39}. Verify financials, legal and compliance, customer concentration, technical risk, governance and fraud.

A one-page memo: yes or no, and why

6If I joined

My first 90 days.

First learn how Saama decides. Then map the four spaces, talk to the people who buy, and bring the partners decisions, not just research.

The bar under each phase shows where it sits in the 12 weeks. Phases overlap on purpose.

Weeks 1–3Learn how Saama decides
  • Read past investment memos and pass notes to learn the bar
  • Sit in on partner meetings and founder calls
  • Learn the rules: cheque size, ownership targets, follow-on reserves, what Saama won’t do
  • Ask partners which past calls they’d make differently, and why

OutputA one-page “How Saama decides” note, checked by a partner

Weeks 3–6Map the four spaces
  • Track 60–80 early companies across the four theses: stage, last round, named customers, signs of paid conversion
  • Refresh the map every two weeks
  • Score the top 15 with the five questions

OutputA live market map and a ranked shortlist

Weeks 6–10Talk to buyers and founders
  • Speak to about 15 buyers: CIOs, plant heads, hospital buyers, AI lab data leads
  • Ask what they tried, what stalled and what they’d pay for
  • Take 25 first calls and go deep on 5

OutputFive evidence files

Weeks 8–12Measure and decide
  • Collect three numbers from portfolio companies: pilot-to-paid, gross margin at scale, services share
  • Take two full memos to the partners
  • Rewrite each thesis with what I got wrong

OutputTwo decisions and sharper theses

The honest gap: everything here comes from public data. The buyer conversations are the part it’s missing, and the first thing I’d do is test these views against them and against Saama’s own deal flow.
